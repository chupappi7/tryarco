How to focus when working from home

At home, nobody sees you scroll. That's the freedom, and the problem. Here's a setup that gives your workday edges and keeps your phone out of it.

By Thinh Tran, maker of · Updated 20 October 2026

In an office, the room keeps you on task. At home, the only thing between you and an hour of YouTube is you. Remote workers often end up working longer and getting less done: the day blurs, focus breaks into pieces, and the phone fills every gap.

The fix is structure you set once, so you don't have to rely on discipline every hour.

1. Give the workday a start and an end

Pick fixed hours, even if your job is flexible. A start time gets you going; an end time is what keeps work from leaking into the evening. Mark both on your plan.

2. Shut the feeds during work hours

Every phone check costs more than the minute it takes, because getting back into the task takes longer. Block the apps that pull you away, only during work hours:

  1. In ARCO, pick the apps under Settings → Apps to block.
  2. Add a Blocked Hours window: Work hours, 9:00–17:00, weekdays.
  3. Allow a set number of breaks. Lunch is yours.

Slack, email, calendar and your work apps stay open. It's only the feeds that close.

3. Protect one or two deep work blocks

Meetings and messages fill whatever time you don't defend. Put one or two blocks of 90 minutes to 2 hours on your timeline for the work that actually moves things. In ARCO, a Deep work habit can start a focus session that blocks apps until it ends, with strict mode if you need it.

4. Plan the day before it starts

Five minutes at the end of today saves the first hour of tomorrow. Write down the top three things, give each a time and end the day knowing where to begin. Here's a five-minute routine.

5. Make the end of the day real

  • Shut down: close the laptop, write tomorrow's top three, done.
  • Change the room or go outside, even for ten minutes.
  • Keep evenings for you: a wind-down window at night stops "just checking" from turning into another hour on the phone.
Tip: put habits like a walk, lunch away from the desk or the gym on the timeline too. If they have a time, they happen.
